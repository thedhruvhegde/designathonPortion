# Impermanence by Design: 30 Case Studies

**Designathon prompt:** *"Design something whose value comes from its temporary nature. Your solution must intentionally embrace disappearance, expiration, transformation, or impermanence as a core feature rather than a limitation."*

Compiled 2026-11-15. Facts were checked with web search. Many stats come from company claims or third-party estimates, and those are flagged as such. Re-check any number before quoting it on a slide.

**Mechanic key:** **Disappear**: content or object vanishes. **Expire**: access or validity ends on a timer. **Transform**: it becomes something else. **Decay**: it degrades physically or biologically.

## Cross-cutting patterns (transferable design principles)

1. **Temporariness lowers the stakes, and low stakes unlock honesty.** Snapchat, Stories, BeReal and Signal all work because people share more freely when it won't last forever. Ask *what fear of permanence* your users have.
2. **Design the ending as an event, not a fade.** Mandala sweeping, the Temple burn, Banksy's shredder and the anger-paper trash bin all show that a *witnessed, ritual* ending creates meaning. A silent deletion doesn't.
3. **A shared clock creates community.** Wordle, BeReal and hanami synchronise everyone to the same window, which turns individual use into a collective ritual and a conversation topic.
4. **Make expiry legible and honest.** Mimica's bumps and VVM's colour square replace guessed dates with a visible, physical state. They make time *readable* (and tactile, which also makes them accessible).
5. **Transformation beats disappearance.** Seed paper becomes plants, Kintsugi turns breakage into gold, and Banksy's shred turns into value. When something ends *into* something else, people feel gain, not loss.
6. **The transition phase is the riskiest part.** Absorb's thrombosis while dissolving, Ephemeral's tattoos that didn't fade, and PVA's unverified end-of-life all show that the end-of-life must be engineered and proven as rigorously as the use phase.
7. **Ephemerality is a feature that is easy to copy, not a moat.** Stories cloned Snapchat, and Fleets failed. Scarcity and expiry need a *reason* rooted in the context (privacy, grief, authenticity), or they become gimmicks.
8. **Defaults decide adoption.** Signal's opt-in disappearing messages were niche until a default timer was offered. Make impermanence the default path, and let permanence be the opt-in.
9. **Match lifespan to the job.** Ooho lasts seconds, the transient pacemaker weeks, Icehotel a season, and a mandala days. The best designs size the object's life exactly to the need it serves, with no excess.
10. **Novelty-based FOMO decays fast.** Clubhouse and BeReal show that urgency without a growing payoff burns out. Pair the "now or never" mechanic with a lasting residue (memory, artefact, relationship).

## White space (gaps for impermanent design)

1. **Self-composting campus flyers and event signage.** Campuses are covered in posters that outlive their events by months. A seed-paper or mycelium-board poster system that *visibly fades or sprouts after the event date* (plus a digital twin that auto-expires from noticeboards) is a gap that no one has filled. Mimica-style honest expiry hasn't been applied to information.
2. **Digital "burn ritual" for stress and rumination (students).** The Nagoya study shows that physically discarding angry writing works, but the authors say the *digital* version is untested. An app or campus kiosk where students write worries that visibly dissolve, burn or compost, perhaps feeding a shared, anonymous "garden," is unbuilt and testable during a designathon.
3. **Auto-decaying digital clutter (files, screenshots, group chats, cloud photos).** Ephemerality exists for *messages* but not for the bulk of digital hoarding: the thousands of screenshots, duplicate downloads and dead group chats. A "files that age" system (thumbnails fade, then compost to a summary unless "watered") applies mono no aware to storage, saving energy and attention.
4. **Expiring shared spaces and objects for transient communities.** Freshers' week, study groups, hall-floor chats and lab rotations are temporary communities that use permanent tools. A space that is *born with an end date* (a semester-long shared board that ends with a generated "yearbook" artefact) gives transformation instead of a lingering dead group.
5. **Honest-expiry indicators for campus food sharing and dorm fridges.** Food-sharing and community fridges are held back by "is this still OK?" doubts. A cheap, Mimica/VVM-style time-temperature sticker for leftovers (or reusable tupperware lids that change colour) is a clear sustainability gap aimed at young people. It also reduces waste and the stigma of eating shared food.

### Source caveats

- Snapchat, Instagram, BeReal and Clubhouse user figures come from company disclosures or third-party app-analytics estimates (Sensor Tower, Apptopia, Similarweb), which often disagree.
- Notpla, Loliware, Mimica and Ecovative figures are largely company or press claims, not independent audits.
- The right-to-be-forgotten totals are dated (2018–2019). Check Google's live Transparency Report for current numbers.
- The VVM "billions of vials" figure is widely repeated but was **not** verified here.
- Supreme's 2024 sale to EssilorLuxottica is from background knowledge, not re-verified in this pass.
