03 · THE INSIGHT
Why impermanence is the value

A permanent listing rots.
A melting one moves.

A fixed price is a promise nobody keeps: it waits, unchanged, until the dumpster wins. A price with a visible end makes everyone act, and gives the ending somewhere to go.

Pattern · sand mandala, Temple burnThe ending is witnessedEvery price falls in public, on one shared curve.
Pattern · vaccine vial monitor, MimicaExpiry made visibleThe curve is the label. You can read the time left.
Pattern · seed paper, kintsugiTransform, don't vanish$0 isn't the end. The item becomes a gift, then a donation.
Pattern · Wordle, hanamiA shared clockMove-out week becomes a campus ritual, not a solitary panic.
05

Here's our insight. A permanent listing rots: it sits at the same price until the dumpster wins. A melting one moves.

From thirty case studies we took four patterns: make the ending a witnessed event, make expiry visible, transform instead of vanishing, and give everyone one shared clock.

The math. A plain exponential never hits zero, so we normalise it to the deadline: the price is exactly p-zero at listing and exactly zero at move-out.

Sellers choose a temperament (gentle, steady or fast) and see the curve before they post.

05 · THE MATH

Watching adds heat

Math · 02 / 04 · heat slows the clock, never stops it
H(t) = Σtᵢ<t e−(t−tᵢ)/τ

Every view, watch or bid is a Hawkes-style event with 6-hour memory (τ = 6 h).

ds/dt =11 + βH(t)β = 0.15

A hot item's clock runs slower, so its price holds while people are interested.

s*(t) = (1−ω)s + ωt,ω = ((t−(T−24))/24)²+

Deadline guarantee. In the last 24 h, s is blended back to wall time, so s*(T) = T and the price still reaches $0 on time.

Watching adds heat. Every view or bid is a Hawkes-style event that slows the item's clock.

But heat can't freeze it: in the last twenty-four hours we blend back to wall time, so it still hits zero on time.

05 · THE MATH

When should I claim?

Math · 03 / 04 · optimal stopping

Demand is learned live: arrivals are Poisson with a Gamma prior, so the posterior mean is λ = (α₀+n)/(β₀+t). Each arrival buys with q(p) = σ(a − b·p/p₀), giving the hazard that someone else claims first, h(t) = λ·q(p(t)).

Waiting saves −p′ per hour but risks losing an item worth v to you. Stop at the first t* where

−p′(t*) ≤ h(t*) · (v − p(t*))
Claim gauge · r(t) = h(v−p) / (−p′)r = 0.00
Wait—
Buyer values the lamp at v = $60 · $/hour—
09

For buyers, waiting saves money but risks losing the item. We learn demand with a Gamma–Poisson posterior and tell you the moment the risk outweighs the savings.

That crossing is your optimal stop, and the gauge flips to Claim Now.

05 · THE MATH

At $0: fairness, then a truck

Math · 04 / 04 · the ending, engineered
Kindness Window · Gale–Shapley stable matching2 h

Claimants rank items (distance, then category). Items rank claimants by need, N = Σwⱼ·flagⱼ + ε, with a seeded lottery ε, so it isn't first-come-first-served.

Donation · Clarke–Wright savings + 2-opt10 dorm stops

S(i,j) = d(0,i) + d(0,j) − d(i,j), merged by descending savings while load ≤ Q; 2-opt reverses (i..j) while d(a,b)+d(c,d) > d(a,c)+d(b,d). Distances in km on a 10 × 6 km campus grid. melt/MATH.md §5–6

10

At zero, fairness takes over. Gale–Shapley matches items to students by need score, with a lottery tie-break so it's not first-come-first-served, and the result is stable.

Whatever's left becomes truck stops: Clarke–Wright savings plus 2-opt cut this route in half.
